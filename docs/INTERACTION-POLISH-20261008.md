## Dropped magazines

User report: "magazines do not fall realistically" and "mags still disappear when
released" (also `reports/live-reload-notes-20261009-023948/notes.json`: "Released
magazine disappears without visible fall").

### Why they vanished

`reports/native-trace-20261009-021010-472/native-trace.json` (XM8, physical weapon
authority active):

- `physical_weapon_authority.weapons[0].magazines`: all seven magazines ended in
  location 4 (`PhysicalMagazineLocation::Dropped`), so the authority applied the
  DropMagazine commands.
- `dropped_magazines`: `dropped 0, landed 0`, floor counters all zero. No prop was
  ever spawned; the 6503 published frames are the amber hint ghost only.
- `gun-events.jsonl`: each release shows the hand claim released one gather before
  the `WellEmpty` transition, and the magazine pack row drops to `magazine:0` on
  that gather.

The presentation (`DroppedMagazineStep`) spawned a prop only if, on the single gather
that saw the receipt, the receipt was not "abandoned", the probe phase was already
`WellEmpty` and the renderer's raw contact (N-1, published by the rig worker) was
valid. Raw contact is exactly what goes missing around a claim release, and a missed
gather was never retried. The weapon's magazine bone is hidden while the well is
empty, so the magazine disappeared from the hand with nothing drawn in its place. A
cancel between submit and receipt also suppressed the presentation even though the
ledger still moved the magazine to Dropped.

### Fix

- `Bc2PhysicalWeaponInteraction::Drain` reports `physicalDrop` for every applied
  DropMagazine, abandoned or not; only the eject (from-well) origin depends on the
  gesture surviving.
- `src/games/bc2/Bc2DroppedMagazineSpawn.h` latches the receipt. The drop starts
  from the last carried magazine pose (retained 500 ms), else the seated well pose
  for a button eject, else the current wrist; with none of them it waits up to
  300 ms for contact and then counts a loss (`spawn.lost`) instead of failing
  silently. Linear and angular velocity come from the wrist history (newest sample
  30..200 ms back; angular velocity is half the summed cross products of the old
  and new basis axes over dt).
- `include/fvr/interaction/DroppedProps.h` gains a box rigid-body model: gravity,
  spin integration, an impulse at the mean of the corners touching the floor
  (restitution only above 0.4 m/s, so slow contacts do not jitter), Coulomb
  friction, rolling resistance while touching, and a settle that lays the box on
  the face nearest the floor with its lowest corner exactly on the floor. Point
  props (zero extents) keep the original M6 behaviour bit for bit.
- Lifetime 30 s and four live magazines (the oldest is recycled) by default; the
  native magazine bone draw and the host instances both use the configured
  lifetime. The visibility cull is relaxed from 3 m / 0.5 m floor mismatch to
  8 m / 1 m. In-game depth-tested drawing (`DrawInGameProp`) is unchanged.

Accounting is unchanged: the prop owns no ammunition. Dropped magazines stay
unavailable in the authority ledger; retained pocket ammunition may still be pooled.
Prop expiry or recycling does not change the ledger.

### Tuning

`interaction::MagazineDropConfig()` in `include/fvr/interaction/DroppedProps.h`:

| Field | Value | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| gravity | 9.81 m/s^2 | |

## Interaction zones, ranges and magnetism

### Audit (before this change)

| Interaction | Code | Capture / release | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Magazine removal grab | `Bc2PhysicalWeaponInteraction::Tick`, `DetachableMagazineConfig` | 0.09 m grab, 0.15 m lateral pull breakaway | Grab is a fresh-grip edge, not hysteresis |

| Pouch | `Bc2MagazinePhysicalReload::DefaultPouch`, `ChestAmmoSupply` | 0.18 m (chest 0.16 m) | Same radius for taking and for returning a magazine |
| Holsters / body slots | `BodyAnchors.h` | chest 0.16 m, shoulders 0.17 m | |
| Ground pickup | `GroundPickup.h`, `Bc2GroundPickup.h` | none | Portable transaction only; BC2 has no runtime adapter (capabilities all false) |

Each range lived in its own file, and no two used the same rules.

### One table

`include/fvr/interaction/InteractionZones.h`, `DefaultInteractionZones()`:

| Zone | Capture m | Release m | Cone (capture / release) | Grip on / off | Magnet in / out | Hover cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Support | 0.20 | 0.32 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 60 / 100 ms | no |
| MagazineGrab | 0.12 | 0.18 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 50 / 100 ms | yes |
| MagazineInsert | 0.08 | 0.16 | 65 / 85 deg | 0.60 / 0.35 | none (the rail guides it) | no |
| ChargingHandle | 0.10 | 0.12 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 40 / 100 ms | yes |
| Pouch | 0.20 | 0.26 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 80 / 100 ms | yes |
| Holster | 0.20 | 0.26 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 80 / 100 ms | yes |
| GroundPickup | 0.25 | 0.35 | none | 0.60 / 0.35 | 80 / 120 ms | yes (no adapter) |

Sources for the values: KF2-VR's `KF2-reload-and-BC2-env-report.md` and
`FrostbiteVR-vs-KF2VR-AS2-reload-assessment.md` (AS2 belt r 0.20 m, magwell 65 / 85
degrees, two-hand engage 25 ms and release 100 ms, pickup blend 80 ms, grip at 0.5).
`src/games/bc2/Bc2InteractionZones.h` binds the table to BC2. A zone only ever
widens a profile (`ZonedRadius`, `ZonedInsertion`, `ZonedHandle`,
`ZonedBodyAnchors`) and never narrows it. A widened magwell rail is re-validated
with `ValidateReloadInsertionProfile`, and the original profile is kept if the
widened one fails validation.

Bindings:

- SupportGrip takes `SupportGripConfig` (capture, release, squeeze on and off).
  Its defaults are the old values; BC2 passes the Support zone.
- `Bc2PhysicalWeaponInteraction::ConfigureZones` sets the magazine grab radius,
  the pull breakaway, the magwell rail (applied when the next weapon or profile
  binds), the charging-handle grab and lateral release, and the pouch radius.
  Returning a held magazine uses the larger release radius, so a magazine released
  at the pouch edge is retained instead of dropped.
- `EnableBodyInventory` widens the chest and shoulder anchors to the Holster
  capture radius.
- Hover cues: an empty, open left hand entering the magazine, charging-handle or
  pouch zone gets one `ReloadHover` tick (0.1 / 12 ms). Entry uses the zone
  hysteresis, so staying in the zone does not repeat it. This addresses the
  headset note that the user could not find the belt or the handle. It is not a
  visible glow; handle geometry is not in the body-ammo cache.

### Magnetism

`ContactMagnet` (InteractionZones.h), used by `Bc2RigPublication` for the support
hand. While support is held, the visible left wrist eases from the tracked hand onto
the authored support contact with a smoothstep over 60 ms. Rotation blends by
shortest-arc quaternion slerp and translation by lerp.

On release, ownership ends at once, as it did before. Only the last visual
correction decays over 100 ms, and it decays in the tracked hand's frame, as the
`docs/floating-hands-contacts.md` contract requires. The returning hand therefore
never follows the gun.

A re-grab partway through the return starts from the visible pose, so the hand does
not pop. Contact, steering and grab decisions all use the raw tracked wrist, so the
magnet cannot sustain a grab by itself. Counters: `support_magnet_blend_poses`,
`support_magnet_return_poses`.

### Support cancellation on weapon flicks

Before this change, any Next/PreviousWeapon action cancelled the support grip,
including a flick that did not switch weapons, so the support hand fell off. Now only the
support release side changed: a flick no longer cancels. A real switch changes
`owner.weapon`, and SupportGrip releases on that identity change (reason Identity,
no fade). The switching trigger and its gating are untouched; they belong to the
switching lane. `support_flick_kept` counts flicks that would previously have
dropped a held support grip.

## Authored contact poses

### What it does

`include/fvr/interaction/AuthoredContacts.h` defines a runtime library of authored
hand contacts. Each contact is a wrist-in-part transform plus 15 fingers-in-wrist
transforms, taken from the native clip's own same-frame samples. Following
`docs/floating-hands-contacts.md`:

- C = authored wrist world * inverse(authored part world)
- F[j] = authored finger world * inverse(authored wrist world)
- Bound wrist = C * part world
- Bound finger[j] = F[j] * bound wrist

`Bc2RigPublication` uses the library for the support hand:

- Wrist. A compiled reviewed profile (`FVR_BC2_AUTHORED_*_HEADER`) keeps priority,
  then the measured support profile, then the library contact, then the current
  native wrist as the old fallback.
- Fingers. When support is attached and the contact carries fingers, the 15
  finger joints are posed from F on the final solved wrist. Finger tips that were
  already written are carried with their previous local relation. Without a
  contact, the native fingers stay as before.

The magnet (above) blends onto that authored wrist.

Lookup is exact: asset name, contact kind, hand and skeleton fingerprint must
match, and the part bone must exist exactly once in the live rig. A part other
than the native weapon bone is re-expressed relative to it through the current
native rig. Anything else falls back.

### Private data: never in git

The samples are a transformation of game animation data. The runtime reads them
from a private text file (format `fvr-authored-contacts 1`):

| Variable | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FVR_AUTHORED_CONTACTS` | `authored-contacts-v1.txt` beside `BC2NativeProbe.dll` | path to the private library; `0` disables it (kill switch: existing IK/native poses only) |

A missing or invalid file means fallback. Any malformed line rejects the whole
file, so partial data is never used. The status appears in the trace's hand
report as `authored_contacts.{state,contacts,error}`, with `authored_support_wrists`,
`authored_support_fingers` and `authored_finger_failures`.

Produce the file with `tools/export_authored_contacts.py` (committed, contains no
game data) from the private intake captures:

```powershell
python -B tools/export_authored_contacts.py --intake reports/floating-hands-intake `
  --asset XM8_sp_s --rig a7f219a1426216ab --out reports/floating-hands-intake/authored-contacts-v1.txt
# then copy it beside the deployed BC2NativeProbe.dll, or set FVR_AUTHORED_CONTACTS to its path
```

It exports:

- support (left wrist in `jntWpn_1` plus fingers, from HandsIkPose)
- gun_grip (right, from HandsIkPose)
- magazine (left wrist in `jntWpn_6` plus fingers, from 1P_Reload). The sample is
  the most magazine-motion 0.2 s window with the wrist within 1 cm / 0.15 of its
  start, the same criteria as `tools/bc2_authored_magazine_geometry.py`.

No handle contact is exported: the reload clip does not establish a hand on the
charging handle.

## Floating hands presentation mode

| Variable | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FVR_HANDS_MODE` | `ik` | `floating` collapses the tracked arms; `ik` (or unset, or any unknown value, reported as `hands_mode_invalid`) keeps today's arms |

The default stays `ik`. Floating cannot be proven to render from CPU tests, and the
native glove draw/owner/palette/layout proof listed in
`reports/floating-hands-intake/NATIVE-DRAW-CONTRACT.md` is still outstanding. That
proof is not claimed here.

### What floating mode does

`src/games/bc2/Bc2FloatingHands.h`, `CollapseArmsToWrists`:

- After the final pose plan is chosen, the skin palette entries of each tracked arm
  (the shoulder subtree minus the wrist subtree: upper arm, forearm, roll and twist
  bones) become a zero basis plus the final wrist translation. Every arm vertex,
  whatever its weight, therefore lands on the wrist joint, and the sleeve and arm
  mesh vanish into the cuff.
- Hands, fingers, the weapon and the hidden native leaves keep their planned skins
  byte for byte. Native padding floats are preserved.
- An untracked arm stays byte-for-byte native, as the existing fallback check
  requires.
- This reuses the existing private palette / Pack route, the same 12-scalar
  collapse used to hide a magazine. No new hook, draw call or native call is
  added.

Interaction is identical in both modes:

- the same tracked wrist targets
- the same `HandInteraction` arbiter and zone hysteresis
- the same immutable authored contacts in held-object space, captured before any
  procedural IK or recoil
- no dual wield and no melee

Report fields in the hand report: `hands_mode`, `floating_poses`,
`floating_rejects`, `floating_capped_mesh: not_attempted_native_draw_unverified`.

### Glove evidence (Blender, private intake scene)

Checked by linking `standard-floating-hands-v1.blend` read-only into the running
Blender session, then removing the link; the user's open scene was unchanged and
not dirty. In the original native glove section (1968 vertices), 37 vertices carry
forearm influence (`RightForeArm`, `LeftForeArmRoll`, `RightForeArmRoll`), and 28
of those are forearm-only. In floating mode those cuff vertices are pulled onto
the wrist joint, closing the cuff. The other 1931 glove vertices are unaffected.
The derived capped floating gloves (866 vertices each) have no arm influence at all.

### Not done

- The capped `standard-floating-hands-v1` mesh substitution: replacing glove
  color/depth draws with the exported mesh needs the native draw capture of the
  four Standard sections, the section-local palette map, the input-layout
  semantics, glove PS resources, and a single all-or-fallback decision across both
  eyes. None of this is attempted.
- The arm reach clamp still applies in floating mode, so hands stop at native
  arm length.
- A between-eye fallback to an ordinary magazine, sight or reload base palette
  shows native arms for that frame. Those bases are captured before the collapse.

Tests: `Bc2FloatingHandsTests` (mode switch with default ik and unknown values
falling back, the arm-minus-hand collapse set, wrist-point skins for any vertex,
preserved padding, untouched hand/finger/weapon edits, untracked and hidden-leaf
handling; synthetic rig only).
